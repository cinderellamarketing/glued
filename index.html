One simple process for every contact

Capsule set up for both sides of Glued, so every contact follows the same steps and nobody who said "not now" slips away.

Prepared for David Wilson  ·  Glued
1 Where things stand today▶

Glued is now just you, with two offers that reach different people. Teams Glued takes The Train Challenge, and the Behavioural and Leadership programmes built on it, to HR and L&D leads. Glued Marketing does brand, websites, email and outreach for small business owners. Teams Glued is the one that opens doors, and brand work can follow.

Most of the work starts with networking: two networking groups, events, one-to-ones, free workshops and LinkedIn. Afterwards there is a pile of business cards, a LinkedIn connection and, with luck, a follow-up call. Proposals sit in a spreadsheet marked won, live or lost, with 20 to 40 live at any time and no contact details beside them. Email goes out through Campaign Monitor, where clients and prospects are mixed together.

The people who say "not now" are the ones who disappear, and some of them are worth the most.

Pipedrive and Popcorn both fell out of use. This time the aim is something light enough that it gets used every day.

3 Built on how you sell▶

The demo followed the process you described, and the setup starts from there:

  • Contacts tagged by relationship: Prospect, Client, Past client, Networking, Partner and Keep warm
  • On each person, their persona, where you met and what they are interested in across both offers. That last field is your cross-sell record
  • Teams Glued and Glued Marketing kept separate, each running from a first conversation to a decision
  • One list of client work, ending with aftercare and cross-sell
  • Four tracks: After networking, Proposal follow-up, Keep warm, and Aftercare and cross-sell

You have started working on five personas. Those become the persona list in Capsule, so the right case study goes to the right person.

You want to settle your own process before anything is built. The discovery call is where that happens, and the demo gives you something to react to.

4 After networking, and "not now"▶

After an event, each person goes into Capsule with a note of where you met. The After networking track then sets the steps: connect on LinkedIn, add them to the right email list, suggest a follow-up call, and send one more nudge if there is no reply.

When someone says not now, the Keep warm track takes over for about six months. It sends a case study that matches their persona, a check-in on LinkedIn, an invitation to a free workshop, and finally a call to ask whether now is the right time. They stay in Capsule with a reason, rather than disappearing.

The pile of business cards can be scanned with an app on your phone, with Zapier creating each contact in Capsule. The app is a monthly subscription you can cancel once the pile is cleared.

5 Apollo, LinkedIn and your email lists▶

Apollo and LinkedIn tools such as Waalaxy stay where they are, for the first outreach. When someone replies and a conversation starts, that person comes into Capsule. Your recent Waalaxy campaign is a good example: lots of replies, most of them no, and about five worth following up. Those five are what Capsule is for.

  • A selected list from Apollo can be exported and imported. Bring in the people you are actually talking to, rather than whole lists.
  • From a LinkedIn profile, a tool called Add to CRM creates the contact in Capsule in one click.

Your Campaign Monitor lists move into Capsule as tags, and Transpond sends from there. The two offers keep separate lists, and there is one source of truth, so the email list and the CRM never drift apart.

6 Keeping it simple▶

You said your instinct is to keep thinking "it could do that too", and that a basic system is better than nothing. So the setup starts with the core: contacts, the two offers, client work and the four tracks.

Capsule has more to it, including simple automations, AI features and links to other tools. Those can come later, once the basics are a habit.

2 The points that shaped this proposal▶

Simple beats complete. Popcorn felt complicated because it was also trying to do email marketing, and Pipedrive had too many options for a plan bought without a conversation first. A basic system that gets used beats one that does everything.

Both offers in one place. Seeing Teams Glued and Glued Marketing side by side was the part that helped. They stay separate, with the cross-sell between them visible.

Outreach tools find people, Capsule keeps the conversation. Apollo and LinkedIn do the first contact. Capsule takes over when someone replies.

One list, kept in Capsule with Transpond sending, so email and the CRM are never out of step again. That was the problem with the CRMs you have used before.

The monthly sessions are there because it is easy to understand something on the day and forget it a month later.
